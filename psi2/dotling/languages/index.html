The dotling languages a visual gallery

Each language is an endless stream of colored dots — one dot per token, color = letter. Structure is always hidden: words are just letter sequences with no separators (gray alternating bands below are annotations, not part of the stream), hierarchy and topics are latent, and noise corrupts the surface. The eight languages here are the ones that carry the study's main findings; the original interactive explainer covers the founding eight in playable form.

Legend: alternating bands = hidden words · brackets = hidden phrase/stanza/canto spans · background wash = hidden topic · red ring = substituted (corrupted) dot · white pupil = which allophone surfaced · dashed hollow dot = duration-jitter repeat frame.

Morsel — the clean baseline

512 words of 4–9 dots over an 8-letter alphabet, sampled by a Zipf law and concatenated with no separators. Alternating gray bands mark the hidden word boundaries — the model never sees them. This is the language where the clean tuning peak first appears (optimal horizon n* = 6 ≈ its mean word length).

Smudge — substitution noise

Morsel's exact lexicon, but every dot is replaced by a random letter with probability 0.25 (red-ringed dots). Decoding a word now requires integrating over corruption — the pressure that pushes the optimal horizon outward on long lexicons (smolder: rumble + this noise, peak n* 12–24).

Growl — allophonic ambiguity

Rumble's 8,192 long words (40–56 dots), but each letter surfaces as one of 3 allophones (inner white pupil = which one), and every surface form is shared by two letters — cube-graph construction, so single dots are genuinely ambiguous and only context resolves them. Speech-like many-to-many variability; peak at the disambiguation scale n*≈3–6.

Creole — syntax

Morsel + substitution noise + a bigram grammar: each word type carries a 20-word successor list followed with probability 0.95, so word order is predictable. Grammar extends how far the future stays informative — it raised the peak and pushed it to n*≈12–24, and on long lexicons (ballad, epic) it pulls n* onto the full word scale.

Myth — four nested scales

A recursive hierarchy: 3-dot words → 4-word phrases (purple brackets) → 4-phrase stanzas (gold) → 4-stanza cantos of 192 dots (red), every level Zipf-sampled from a fixed book, plus 25% substitution noise. Four probes on one stream sweep the whole spectrum: word peaks at n=6, phrase at 6–12, stanza at 24–48, canto rises monotonically past n=96.

Saga — a slow hidden state

Morsel-like words, but an invisible 8-state topic (background wash) persists for ~114 words and reweights the whole lexicon. Nothing marks the topic in any single dot — yet topic decodability jumps 0.55 → 0.89 as the training horizon grows from 1 to 24: long horizons force the model to carry slow state. The largest single effect in the study.

Speech — every pressure at once

Topics + a lexicon + allophonic surface variability + duration jitter (dashed hollow dots are repeated frames). The closest dotling analog of real speech: at small scale the model must trade word precision against slow state as the horizon grows; capacity and data soften the trade-off.

Thicket — the full speech analog, hard mode

3,072 words of 6–14 letters over 16 latent letters, each surfacing as one of 4 overlapping allophones (32 surface forms), every token lasting 1–3 frames (variable duration), noisy silence bursts between words that also leak into word interiors, and a mild word grammar — 40 surface tokens, ~18-token words. Word decodability plateaus at n* = 8–20 on a fine-grained horizon sweep, and the label-free discovery pipeline recovers the silence inventory perfectly, segments at F1 0.64, and names units best with a two-model recipe: segment with the n=1 model, embed with the n≈12 model.

Swing — a continuous impostor

No words at all: a smoothly drifting phase, quantized to 8 bins, with persistent hidden velocity and 30% observation noise. It looks structured — but it is memoryless at the right description level, and it is the one kind of stream where longer horizons never help (and the fast velocity readout actively degrades). Discrete latent units, not complexity per se, are what the multi-token objective rewards.
